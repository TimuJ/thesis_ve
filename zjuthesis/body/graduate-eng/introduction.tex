\chapter{Introduction and Background}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro:background}

Video super-resolution (VSR) has advanced rapidly with the adoption of diffusion-based and
transformer-based architectures. Where earlier CNN-based methods upscaled fixed short clips
(typically 7--32 frames), modern methods such as MGLD-VSR \cite{he_mgld_2024},
Upscale-A-Video \cite{zhou_upscale_2024}, and the one-step streaming design FlashVSR
\cite{zhuang_flashvsr_2025} can process substantially longer sequences in latent or
sliding-window fashion, enabling deployment on real-world footage that runs into minutes
rather than seconds.

This shift toward long-video SR introduces an evaluation gap. The standard VSR benchmarks
--- REDS \cite{nah_reds_2019} (100-frame clips at $720\!\times\!1280$), Vimeo-90K
\cite{xue_vimeo90k_2019} (7-frame septuplets), Vid4 \cite{liu_vid4_2014}, UDM10
\cite{yi_pfnl_2019}, and SPMCS \cite{tao_spmcs_2017} --- were designed for short-clip
evaluation and report full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM \cite{wang_ssim_2004}, LPIPS
\cite{zhang_lpips_2018}) against paired HR ground truth. When applied to long videos in
the wild, three fundamental limitations emerge:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No HR ground truth.} Real deployment videos have no paired high-resolution
          reference, making full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS
          \cite{ding_dists_2020}) inapplicable.
    \item \textbf{Adjacent-frame temporal metrics miss long-range artefacts.} Existing
          temporal metrics --- tOF and tLP from TecoGAN \cite{chu_tecogan_2020}, and
          E$^*$warp from DOVE \cite{yang_dove_2024} --- are computed at a single
          adjacent-frame gap ($k{=}1$). A method that is locally smooth but drifts globally
          over minutes is undetectable by these measures.
    \item \textbf{Smoother-output bias in learned representations.} DINOv2-based
          \cite{oquab_dinov2_2023} consistency metrics and LPIPS favour methods whose
          outputs change less in feature space between frames. Diffusion-based methods
          produce detail-rich outputs that score worse on these metrics despite being
          visually superior, a structural bias that misrepresents quality for modern SR
          architectures.
\end{itemize}

More precisely, the evaluation of long-form video super-resolution is structurally
insufficient in four overlapping ways, documented empirically in
Chapter~\ref{ch:problem}. First, \emph{per-frame fidelity metrics} (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS,
DISTS, CLIP-IQA) are temporal-blind by construction: a video that drifts slowly in colour
across thousands of frames, or that jumps at every tile boundary of a tile-based diffusion
super-resolver, or whose face identity degrades from minute one to minute two, scores
identically to a colour-stable, seam-free, identity-preserving video provided each
individual frame is locally crisp. Second, \emph{short-range temporal metrics} (tOF, tLP,
E$^*$warp at adjacent-frame lag $k{=}1$) measure adjacent-frame smoothness but cannot see
consistency across the longer time horizons where the most damaging long-video failures
actually occur, and they systematically favour spatially smoother output. Third,
\emph{perceptual aggregates} (DOVER \cite{wu_dover_2023}, VBench-2.0
\cite{huang_vbench2_2025}) average clip-level scores up to a per-video number, removing
the very cross-clip consistency signal that long-video evaluation requires. Fourth,
\emph{even metrics that should catch a failure mode exhibit content-dependent calibration
shifts} that are not surfaced to the user: the same metric, under identical
hyperparameters, can flip its method ranking on particular content (close-up faces, dense
motion) because a learned classifier is pushed into a different operating regime.

A researcher who wishes to know whether method $A$ is more temporally consistent than
method $B$ on long-form content has, today, no single trustworthy answer. This thesis
addresses that gap.

\section{Research Aim, Hypotheses, and Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:aim}

The primary aim of this research is to develop and validate a no-reference long-range
video consistency metric for long-form video super-resolution. The metric must (i) catch
the long-range failure modes to which existing metrics are demonstrably blind; (ii)
preserve correct per-video method rankings on real super-resolved outputs, including on
content where existing learned metrics flip against human judgement; and (iii) be
reproducible, modular, and extensible. The metric introduced for this purpose is the
\textbf{Long-Range Video Consistency Composite (LR-VCC)}, specified in
Chapter~\ref{ch:method}.

The metric research is organised around three falsifiable hypotheses:

\begin{description}
    \item[H1 (Composition).] Reliability-weighted softmax-log-mean composition of
        independent sub-metrics catches failure modes that no single constituent
        sub-metric catches.
    \item[H2 (Multi-scale temporal).] Aggregating temporal warping error over multiple
        frame gaps catches both high-frequency and long-range temporal failure modes that
        fixed-scale temporal metrics miss.
    \item[H3 (Reliability gating).] Per-sub-metric reliability gates derived from
        interpretable signals reduce content-dependent ranking flips of the type observed
        in the close-up regime-shift case study.
\end{description}

A second research question arose from applying LR-VCC to real models. The best-ranked
method under LR-VCC, the streaming one-step diffusion model FlashVSR, simultaneously
exhibited the worst long-range semantic-drift sub-metric score of the three methods
evaluated --- precisely the failure signature the metric was designed to expose. Because
FlashVSR's streaming design lets absolute rotary position embedding (RoPE) indices grow
without bound as video length increases, position encoding is the natural suspect:

\begin{description}
    \item[RQ (Positional extrapolation).] How does RoPE extrapolate beyond its trained
        window in time and in space in a streaming diffusion VSR model, and is positional
        extrapolation the cause of the observed long-video and high-resolution quality
        degradation?
\end{description}

Chapter~\ref{ch:rope} answers this question causally, using a bit-exact position-injection
instrument that manipulates position indices independently of video content.

This thesis makes five contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A systematic failure-mode characterisation of existing long-video SR
          metrics} (Chapter~\ref{ch:problem}): a content-dependent regime shift in which
          two VBench-2.0 metrics flip rankings against visual judgement on close-up
          content; a temporal-scale crossover in which the tOF winner inverts between
          $k{=}1$ and $k{\geq}10$, demonstrating that adjacent-frame temporal metrics
          measure spatial smoothness rather than consistency; and a severity-response
          study showing that of 32 (metric, artefact family) cells across eight
          widely-used metrics, only five respond cleanly --- with slow colour drift
          invisible to every baseline metric tested.
    \item \textbf{LR-VCC, a no-reference reliability-gated composite metric}
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}): seven sub-metrics covering appearance, multi-scale
          temporal consistency, identity preservation, colour-distribution stability,
          anchored colour stability, semantic trajectory drift, and colour-trajectory
          slope, composed by a reliability-weighted softmax-log-mean. Every sub-metric is
          gated by an interpretable reliability signal, and every design iteration closed
          a specific characterised failure mode.
    \item \textbf{A parameterised synthetic consistency benchmark for long-video SR}
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments}): twelve artefact families (drift, boundary,
          flicker, identity, background, and flip/control families) at five severities
          over five long base videos, with generators verified for zero-severity identity
          and seed reproducibility.
    \item \textbf{A three-method real-model evaluation} (Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments}):
          LR-VCC applied to MGLD-VSR, Upscale-A-Video, and FlashVSR on long real-SR
          outputs, yielding a ranking (FlashVSR $>$ MGLD-VSR $>$ UAV) that per-frame
          metrics do not produce, and exposing FlashVSR's long-range semantic drift.
    \item \textbf{A causal analysis of RoPE extrapolation in streaming diffusion VSR}
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:rope}): absolute-position offsets are quality-free even
          50$\times$ beyond the trained window on every axis; relative-distance geometry
          dilation damages quality monotonically while mild compression is free; the
          model's long-video drift is demonstrably \emph{not} positional; and real spatial
          grid extension to $1.5\times$ costs only $-0.15$~dB while spatial position
          interpolation there \emph{hurts}. These verdicts exonerate position encoding and
          redirect improvement effort toward streaming cache/generation mechanics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:intro:related}

\subsection{Video Super-Resolution Methods}
\label{sec:related:vsr}

Modern VSR methods fall into three architectural families. \emph{Recurrent CNN} methods
such as BasicVSR++ \cite{chan_basicvsrpp_2022} propagate features bidirectionally with
flow-guided alignment; RealBasicVSR \cite{chan_realbasicvsr_2022} extends the family to
real-world degradations and contributes the degradation pipeline used by later
benchmarks. \emph{Transformer} methods --- VRT \cite{liang_vrt_2022} and its recurrent
variant RVRT \cite{liang_rvrt_2022} --- model temporal dependencies with attention but
face quadratic cost in sequence length, restricting them to short clips.
\emph{Diffusion-based} methods leverage generative priors for perceptual quality:
StableSR \cite{wang_stablesr_2024} adapts a text-to-image diffusion prior to image SR;
Upscale-A-Video \cite{zhou_upscale_2024} adds temporal layers for video; MGLD-VSR
\cite{he_mgld_2024} guides latent diffusion with optical-flow motion cues; and FlashVSR
\cite{zhuang_flashvsr_2025} distils a video diffusion transformer \cite{wan_wan_2025}
into a one-step streaming super-resolver capable of arbitrary-length input. The two
primary baselines evaluated throughout this thesis, MGLD-VSR and Upscale-A-Video,
represent the detail-preserving and smoothing ends of the diffusion VSR spectrum
respectively; FlashVSR represents the emerging streaming class. Their architectures are
detailed in Chapter~\ref{ch:foundations}.

\subsection{Video Quality Assessment}
\label{sec:related:vqa}

The existing metric ecosystem can be organised into five families:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full-reference pixel/perceptual} (PSNR, SSIM \cite{wang_ssim_2004},
          LPIPS \cite{zhang_lpips_2018}, DISTS \cite{ding_dists_2020}) --- require HR
          ground truth; inapplicable to deployment-time long-video evaluation.
    \item \textbf{No-reference single-aspect} (CLIP-IQA \cite{wang_clipiqa_2023}, MUSIQ
          \cite{ke_musiq_2021}, NIQE, BRISQUE) --- measure per-frame quality; no temporal
          dimension.
    \item \textbf{Temporal pixel-level} (tOF, tLP from TecoGAN \cite{chu_tecogan_2020};
          E$^*$warp from DOVE \cite{yang_dove_2024}) --- adjacent-frame ($k{=}1$)
          optical-flow consistency; systematically miss long-range temporal structure.
    \item \textbf{Semantic video quality} (VBench \cite{huang_vbench_2024}, VBench-2.0
          \cite{huang_vbench2_2025}) --- multi-aspect benchmarks covering subject
          consistency, Human\_Identity, Human\_Anatomy, and others. These benchmarks were
          designed for text-to-video generation evaluation; thirteen of VBench-2.0's
          eighteen dimensions require text prompts and are therefore inapplicable to
          super-resolution, while the remaining prompt-independent dimensions operate at
          clip level and average per-clip scores up to a single video number, removing
          the cross-clip drift signal that long-video consistency assessment requires.
          Content-dependent failure modes are documented in Chapter~\ref{ch:problem}.
    \item \textbf{Disentangled aesthetic-technical no-reference} (DOVER
          \cite{wu_dover_2023}) --- a learned video quality predictor combining an
          aesthetic branch and a technical branch. DOVER is per-video and produces a
          single aggregate; it does not specifically model cross-frame temporal drift.
\end{enumerate}

No existing metric satisfies all four desiderata simultaneously: (1)~no-reference,
(2)~multi-time-scale temporal coverage, (3)~robust to smoother-output bias from learned
representations, and (4)~reliable across diverse long-video content including close-up
scenes. LR-VCC (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}) is designed to satisfy all four.

\subsection{Position Encodings and Length Extrapolation}
\label{sec:related:rope}

Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) \cite{su_roformer_2024} encode token positions as
rotations applied to query and key vectors, making attention scores a function of
relative position. RoPE underlies most modern large language models and, in a
three-axis factorised form (time, height, width), most video diffusion transformers
including Wan2.1 \cite{wan_wan_2025}, the backbone of FlashVSR. A model trained on a
fixed context window degrades when evaluated beyond it; the language-modelling community
has produced a family of \emph{position interpolation} remedies --- linear position
interpolation (PI) \cite{chen_pi_2023}, NTK-aware frequency scaling \cite{bloc97_ntk_2023},
and YaRN \cite{peng_yarn_2023} --- that compress or re-scale position indices so that
relative distances remain inside the trained range. Whether these findings transfer to
\emph{video} diffusion transformers, where positions are three-dimensional and streaming
inference lets the temporal index grow without bound, has not been systematically studied.
Chapter~\ref{ch:rope} provides such a study for a production streaming VSR model,
decomposing extrapolation into absolute-position magnitude and relative-distance geometry
and measuring each against real ground truth.

\section{Thesis Organisation}
\label{sec:intro:organisation}

Chapter~\ref{ch:foundations} describes the foundational technologies this thesis builds
on: the evaluated VSR models, rotary position embeddings, and the perceptual and temporal
measurement toolkit. Chapter~\ref{ch:problem} characterises the failure modes of existing
metrics on long-video SR content. Chapter~\ref{ch:method} specifies the LR-VCC metric and
its design rationale. Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} validates LR-VCC on the synthetic
benchmark and on three real SR methods. Chapter~\ref{ch:rope} presents the RoPE
extrapolation study. Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} discusses significance and limitations,
and concludes.
