### Problem and Background

- Long-form Video Restoration
    - Restore a degraded low quality video sequence $V^{LQ} = \{I_1^{LQ}, I_2^{LQ}, \dots, I_T^{LQ}\}$ into a high-quality video sequence $V^{HQ} = \{I_1^{HQ}, I_2^{HQ}, \dots, I_T^{HQ}\}$  where $T > 1000$ frames
        - for VSR: $I_t^{LQ} \in \R^{HxWx3}$ and $I_t^{HQ} \in$  $\R^{sH\times sW\times 3}$ (upscaled by a factor of $s$) ⇒ only spatial upscaling is considered here
    - Key Challenges
        1. Long-term temporal consistency   
            - While diffusion based VSR methods restores videos with more details (addressing over-smoothing issue in prior methods), they struggle with **temporal consistency and input-faithfulness(Due to hallucination)**
            - flickering, semantic-consistency
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image.png)
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%201.png)
            
        2. Memory and Latency Efficiency 
        3. Generalisation to arbitrary resolution input 

### Related works

- Long-form VSR is closely related to long video generation in T2V while it also has its own unique challenges and settings
    - both needs to condition on self-generated history to maintain long-term consistency
    - both struggle with **forgetting** as it’s difficult put all the history in the context
    - VSR, unlike, T2V have LQ inputs as extra conditions for structural guidance of a particular frame
    - in VSR, the semantics and structure of the whole video, can be inferred from the LQ input
- Challenges in long-form T2V generation
    1. Bi-directional attention between history frames and current generation suffers from 
        - in-efficiency especially, both latency and memory, for long videos
        - prevent interactive applications and streaming as current frame depends on future frame
        
        <aside>
        💡
        
        to enforce long-form consistency, the model has to maintain consistency between history frames and current to-be generated frame, thus, it needs to condition on history frames
        
        </aside>
        
    - Autoregressive Video Diffusion models(AR-VDM), frames have causal relation-ship, are used to address (a):
        
        ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%202.png)
        
        - AR-VDM are prone to **exposure/observation bias** that causes error accumulation that leads to quality drift as the length of video sequence increases  ****
            
            > Exposure/Observation-bias: **input mismatch between training and inference** where at training  ground truth training samples are used as the input context but at inference self generated outputs are used as input
            > 
            
            > Error accumulation: each generated frame builds on potentially flawed previous frames that causes prediction errors to magnify and worsen over-time
            > 
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%203.png)
            
            - Self-forcing addresses this by Using self-roll out during training
        - Training-Test gap in long video: models are trained on short videos while at inference they are tasked to generate long videos
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%204.png)
            
            - Long-live addresses this by rolling-out long videos at inference but supervise short videos (to avoid OOM)
    - Forgetting due to limited context window  ⇒ preserving more history frames in context easily becomes computationally expensive(with bi-directional) or hit OOM (with AR-VDM), therefore,  **effective memory management is necessary**
        - FramePack ⇒ **compresses** history context with frame-wise importance(time and feature metrics) in a fixed context length
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%205.png)
            
        - MemoryV2V: Memory + Retrieval + Efficient Conditioning
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%206.png)
            
            - it compares Dino-v2 embedding similarities between **source(not the edited)** video of history frames and current frame to retrieve relevant history frames
                - in addition to the retrieved frames, it keeps nearby frames for short-term memory(smoothness)
                - Long-term memory (for semantic consistency) + short-term memory(for video smoothness)
                
                ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%207.png)
                
        - Rolling forcing uses sink tokens ⇒ but this might be relevant to VSR
            
            ![image.png](Week%201%20Feb%2023%20-%20Mar%204/image%208.png)
            
- Take-away
    - Most of the works in long-form video generation focus on addressing **quality drift** due to exposure bias and enable **streaming** to make the model interactive for live user prompts
        - quality drift might not be an issue in VSR as we have LQ for structural guidance unlike to T2V
            
            <aside>
            💡
            
            we need to test existing works for this  ⇒ it exists
            
            </aside>
            
        - Streaming or Efficiency, although appealing, might not be our target for this work
    - However, context management to avoid **forgetting** is the key challenge that VSR share with T2V
    - Moreover, we can also share the training efficiency techniques followed in these works to improve training efficiency which is necessary for VSR
